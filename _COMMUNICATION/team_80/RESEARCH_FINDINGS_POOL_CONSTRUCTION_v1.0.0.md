## 1. Shell construction approaches for a private, dug residential pool

The client's own wording ("dug/excavated") plus the freeform/curved (kidney-like) shape in his hand sketch both
point toward an **in-situ shell built to a custom curve**, not a prefab drop-in unit.

| Approach | What it is | Fit for a freeform/curved private pool | Realistic maintenance profile |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Poured concrete / gunite-shotcrete shell** | Rebar cage sprayed with sand-cement mix, shaped freehand, then finished | Excellent — the standard method for genuinely custom/freeform shapes; matches "dug" directly | Shell durable (decades); the **finish coat** is the real maintenance driver (below); 3–6 month build |
| **Concrete masonry block (CMU) shell** + waterproofing + plaster/tile | Blockwork in the excavation, rendered/waterproofed, then finished | Good for gentle curves, more constrained on tight freeform curvature than sprayed concrete, but common regionally | Same finish-driven profile as concrete; often more budget-accessible route to full tile |
| **Vinyl liner over engineered wall panels** | Steel/polymer panels in excavation, vermiculite/sand base, liner secured over top | Weaker fit — cheaper/faster (2–3 wks vs. 3–6 mo) but suited to simpler shapes; tight freeform curves possible but pricier custom seaming | Smooth surface resists algae well, but liner is a recurring cost — typical replacement ~7–15 yrs, vulnerable to root/debris/claw punctures |
| **Fiberglass shell** | Prefab one-piece composite, craned into excavation | Weakest fit — shape limited to catalog molds and access/transport size; unlikely to reproduce the freeform sketch, and this plot's access constraints (existing gate/fence, established trees) make craning a real question mark | Very low day-to-day maintenance (gelcoat resists algae, no replastering) — worth naming if client turns out flexible on shape, but cuts against "dug" as described |

**Given the brief, the realistic near-term candidates are poured-concrete/gunite or block-built shells** — the
choice between them is a contractor/cost/regional-availability question, not a landscape-design one.

### Surface finish — where most of the long-term maintenance burden actually lives

Regardless of shell method, the **interior finish** drives algae tendency, chemical-balance sensitivity, and
resurfacing frequency far more than the shell material itself:
- **Plaster (marcite/quartz-aggregate):** lowest upfront cost, least durable — commonly cited ~7–12 years before
  replastering; most algae/stain-prone and most sensitive to water-chemistry swings. Given "low maintenance is
  priority #1," plain plaster is the **weakest fit** despite being cheapest.
- **Pebble/aggregate finish:** denser/smoother once cured, meaningfully better algae resistance, longer service
  life — better long-term low-maintenance value despite higher upfront cost.
- **Tile (ceramic/porcelain/glass):** least porous, easiest to clean, most algae/stain-resistant, most durable
  (properly installed) — but most expensive; grout lines need epoxy grout to avoid becoming a grime trap.
  **Regional-practice note:** fully-tiled pool interiors are, in general professional knowledge, very common in
  Israeli/Mediterranean residential construction — more so than the American plain-plaster default — but this
  specific regional-practice claim could not be verified against a citable source this pass; **flag as a question
  for a local Israeli pool contractor**, not an established fact here.
- **Separately:** the sanitation system (chlorine dosing vs. salt-chlorination vs. UV/ozone-assisted) is an
  independent, often bigger maintenance driver than shell/finish — connects directly to §3 below.

## 2. Pool-surround / coping materials — fitting kurkar/basalt + "play of heights," avoiding "moon rock"

- **Basalt paving/coping** — dark, dense, very durable, directly matches the client's preference; flamed or
  bush-hammered (textured, non-slip) finish is standard in Mediterranean-climate residential work.
- **Travertine** — pale, textured, naturally slip-resistant when honed/tumbled, stays relatively cool underfoot in
  summer sun — a very common Mediterranean pool-coping stone, though lighter-toned than basalt/kurkar.
- **Kurkar — a genuine caveat, not a straightforward yes:** locally beloved (Acre/Jaffa sea walls) but a soft,
  porous, aeolian calcareous sandstone. Documented deterioration studies on kurkar seawalls show accelerated
  weathering under sustained wet/humid/salt-exposed conditions — exactly the cycling a pool coping edge
  experiences (splash-out, chemical exposure, constant wet-dry cycling, bare-foot traffic). **Recommendation to
  raise, not decide:** use kurkar for what it's naturally good at (retaining walls, stepped terracing, raised
  planting beds — the "play of heights"), reserve a harder stone (basalt, travertine, or a kurkar-toned
  porcelain/composite paver) for the actual coping/waterline edge.
- **Avoid:** large white rounded "moon rock" boulders. Worth flagging: large dry-stacked boulder groupings right
  at a pool edge also create the exact cavity/hiding-spot problem the client raised about snakes (§7) — one more
  reason to lean toward tight-fitting cut/dressed coping rather than piled boulders near water.
- **Achieving "play of heights" without boulders:** stepped kurkar/basalt retaining walls, varied-height planted
  terraces, and a basalt/travertine coping band at the water's edge.

## 3. Biological/non-chlorine pool & the swim-pond hybrid option — directly relevant to the open pond question

Presented as a **genuine option to raise with the client, not a recommendation** — maps directly onto his own
still-open question (`CLIENT_BRIEF` §4/§9): a separate ornamental/fish pond in addition to the confirmed pool?

**The technique is real and established.** Natural swimming pools/"swim ponds" (German *Naturpool*/*Schwimmteich*
tradition) split the water body into two connected zones: a **swim zone** (open water, ~1.8–2.5m deep at the deep
point, chemical-free) and a **regeneration/planted zone** (shallow, ~20–60cm, gravel-substrate, aquatic/marginal
plants — bacteria + plants do biological filtration, typically assisted by a low-power pump cycling every 6–24
hours). **Sizing rule of thumb:** the regeneration zone commonly needs ~half the swim zone's surface area
(sometimes ~a third on larger designs) — a real footprint cost, not just a construction-method cost.

**Honest maintenance picture — cuts both ways against the client's #1 priority:**
- *In favor:* no chlorine dosing/testing regime.
- *Against it (stated plainly since low-maintenance is a hard constraint):* not zero-maintenance — periodic
  aquatic-plant trimming, occasional sludge/biofilm removal, skimmer/pump upkeep, typically a 1–3 year
  "establishment period" with common algae blooms while the biological system matures. Generally needs **more
  total footprint** than a same-size conventional pool. Water looks visibly greener/more pond-like — a genuinely
  different aesthetic the client hasn't been asked about.

**Two ways this connects to his open question — present both, don't pick one:**
1. A combined swim-pond could satisfy both needs in one system — *if* he's comfortable with the aesthetic and
   footprint.
2. Keeping them fully separate — a conventional (chlorine/salt) pool (most directly serves low-maintenance for
   swimming, easiest to size/engineer with certainty) plus a small, separate ornamental/fish pond (much smaller
   volume, straightforward recirculating-pump-plus-biofilter-and-UV-clarifier category) — fully decouples
   swim-water-quality risk from decorative-pond aesthetics.

Which fits depends on information not yet available: how he feels about non-clear water, how much footprint he's
willing to give up, and how important having both in one system is versus separate simple builds.

## 4. Climate & safety factors specific to this project

**Climate (Csa hot-summer Mediterranean):** essentially rainless, high-30s°C summers May–Oct drive both high
evaporation (ongoing refill load) and elevated algae-growth pressure. **Pool siting and shading are themselves a
maintenance lever, not just aesthetic:** full prolonged summer sun → warmer/more algae-friendly water, faster
chlorine off-gassing; some afternoon shade reduces algae/evaporation but introduces leaf/fruit litter — a real
coordination point given the client's preferred fruit trees (avocado, mango, pecan, carob, bananas, `CLIENT_BRIEF`
§5) should be sited away from dropping into the pool/regeneration zone. A **pool cover** is a genuine,
shell/finish-independent lever cutting evaporation, debris, and algae load simultaneously.

**Safety — snake/wildlife concern (§7) interacts directly with surround material choice:** tight-fitting,
mortared/close-jointed coping minimizes crevices loose dry-stacked stone creates — consistent with both the
"no moon-rock piles" preference and the snake-avoidance request. Keeping dense groundcover/rock features set back
from the pool edge/walkways serves both low-maintenance and safety. **A genuine tension worth surfacing, not
resolving:** the swim-pond's planted, damp, gravel regeneration zone is inherently a vegetated micro-habitat that
tends to attract more general wildlife (amphibians, insects, and what eats them) right at poolside — independent
of snakes specifically, this cuts somewhat against minimizing wildlife-attracting features near used areas; name
this explicitly if the swim-pond option is raised with him.
