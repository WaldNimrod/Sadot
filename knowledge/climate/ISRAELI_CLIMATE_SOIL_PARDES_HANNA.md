# Israeli climate + soil — Pardes Hanna area (general, non-plot-specific)

**Scope caveat (read first):** everything below is regional/general knowledge about the Pardes Hanna-Karkur area. It
is NOT a substitute for a real site survey. `raw-materials/from-client/` is currently empty — no plot-specific
topography, drainage, sun/shade map, or lab soil test exists yet. Treat every fact below as a starting hypothesis to
confirm once real data arrives (see `SDT-S001-P002-WP004`, currently BLOCKED).

## 1. Location + climate classification

Pardes Hanna-Karkur sits in Israel's central coastal plain (the Sharon region), inland from the Mediterranean coast
(roughly 8-10 km), in Haifa District. Köppen climate classification: **Csa — hot-summer Mediterranean climate**.

- **Summers (roughly May-October):** hot and essentially rainless; daytime highs commonly in the low-to-mid 30s °C,
  high humidity near the coast, moderating somewhat further inland.
- **Winters (roughly November-April):** mild and wet; most of the region's annual rainfall falls in this window,
  with occasional cold snaps but rare frost at this latitude/elevation.
- **Annual rainfall:** approximate regional range 500-650mm/year for the northern Sharon, concentrated Oct-Apr,
  essentially zero in summer — meaning any garden design must plan for irrigated dry-season survival, not just
  established-plant rainfall dependence.
- **Practical design implication:** plant palette should weight heavily toward Mediterranean-climate-adapted species
  (drought-tolerant once established, tolerant of a rainless 5-6 month summer) unless irrigation capacity is
  explicitly budgeted for thirstier species.

## 2. Typical regional soil

The Sharon coastal-plain belt (which includes the Pardes Hanna area) is characterized by:

- **"Hamra" soil** (חמרה) — a reddish-brown sandy clay loam, one of Israel's most common coastal-plain soil types,
  typically formed over **kurkar** (a local calcareous/aeolian sandstone bedrock common along this belt).
- General properties where hamra is present: moderately well-drained, workable texture (not heavy clay), typically
  mildly acidic to near-neutral pH, historically favored for citrus and orchard agriculture in this region (Pardes
  Hanna's own name — "Pardes" = orchard/citrus grove — reflects this area's historic citrus-growing identity).
- **Caveat:** soil composition varies at the parcel level (fill history, prior landscaping, construction disturbance
  around an existing house are all common local confounders) — this is a regional prior, not a substitute for an
  actual soil test once the client survey arrives.

## 3. What this means for plant selection (ties to `knowledge/crops/PLANT_SELECTION_STARTER.md`)

- Favor species tolerant of: hot dry summers, moderate winter rainfall, near-neutral-to-mildly-acidic well-drained
  soil, and (unless proven otherwise) no unusual frost risk.
- Citrus and other historically-orcharded species have a plausible regional fit (matching the area's agricultural
  history) but final selection must wait for the real sun/shade + drainage map.
- Companion permaculture design considerations (water harvesting for the long dry summer, mulch strategy for hamra's
  moderate drainage) are elaborated in `knowledge/permaculture/03_WATER_AND_SWALES.md`.
